# SJ Cafe — Meeting Prep Pack

Everything you need to walk in and hold your own.

---

## 1. What you already know about his business

Use these. They prove you did homework, which is 80% of why small business owners trust
someone young.

| Fact | Where it came from | Why it matters to your pitch |
|---|---|---|
| On Talabat, Drivu and Google | Public listings | He already pays commission to two platforms |
| 4.1★ / 61 Google reviews | Google | Below the 4.5 his Talabat page shows — his walk-in experience lags his delivery one |
| Top complaints are **wait time** | Google reviews (40 min for 5 drinks, 24 min for 2 bowls) | Pre-order is a fix, not a feature |
| Open 15:00–01:00, seven days | Google | Late-night, so ordering must be phone-first and work in the dark |
| Sells whole bean (Brazil, Ethiopia) | Drivu menu | Retail product = recurring revenue you can unlock |
| Talabat lists Lotus Crepe & Kinder Pancakes; Drivu doesn't | Both platforms | **His two menus disagree.** Open with this. |

That last row is your opener. Something like:

> "Before anything else — your Talabat menu and your Drivu menu don't match. Talabat's
> promoting Lotus Crepe and Kinder Pancakes as best sellers and they're not on your drive-thru
> menu at all. That's the problem I want to solve: right now you're maintaining your menu in
> three places and none of them are yours."

Verify this is actually true before you say it — check both live, in case one has been
updated since.

## 2. Feature ideas — with a recommendation

Do **not** show him this whole list. Owners freeze when given twenty options. Show him
Phase 1, mention Phase 2 exists, keep Phase 3 in your back pocket for the upsell in month four.

### Phase 1 — ship this first (2–3 weeks)

| Feature | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|
| **Bilingual EN/AR menu, proper RTL** | Half his customers read Arabic. Most small cafe sites do Arabic badly or not at all. Cheap for you, visibly impressive to him. |
| **QR codes — different ones per location** | Table, counter, drive-thru window. Different QR per spot means he can see *where* scans come from. That's data Talabat never gives him. |
| **Drive-thru pre-order with car plate + colour** | This is the killer feature. Customer orders from the road, arrives, taps "I'm here", staff walk it out. |
| **"I've arrived" button** | Directly attacks the 40-minute-wait reviews. |
| **Kitchen tablet screen** | No new hardware — any old iPad by the machine. |
| **One-tap sold-out toggle** | Staff will actually use this. Nothing annoys customers more than ordering something that ran out. |
| **Owner edits prices himself** | Removes his biggest fear: "I'll be stuck calling you every time." |
| **Cash on pickup only** | Lets you go live in days instead of waiting weeks for a merchant account. |

### Phase 2 — after it's live and working (2–3 weeks)

- Apple Pay, Google Pay and cards via Stripe
- Simple sales dashboard: revenue today, best sellers, orders by hour
- Automatic receipts by email/SMS

### Phase 3 — the upsells

- **Loyalty stamps by phone number** — 9 drinks, 10th free. No app, no signup. This is the
  single highest-ROI thing on the whole list for a cafe with regulars.
- **Coffee bean subscription** — he already sells Brazil and Ethiopia beans. Monthly recurring
  delivery is pure margin and it's a genuinely differentiated product in Shahama.
- **Google review funnel** — a thank-you message 45 minutes after collection with a review
  link. Moving 4.1 → 4.5 changes how many people click him in Maps.
- **PWA "add to home screen"** — he gets an app icon on customers' phones with no App Store
  fee and no Apple review process. Owners love hearing this.
- **Ramadan / Iftar pre-order mode** — build it in Jan, sell it in Feb.
- **Office catering form** — Shahama has offices; a 20-drink order is worth ten walk-ins.

### Things you should talk him *out* of

If he asks for these, push back. It protects the project and it makes you look senior.

- **Own delivery drivers.** Insurance, permits, no-shows. Let Talabat do delivery; his website
  handles pickup and drive-thru, where he keeps 100%.
- **A native iOS/Android app.** AED 99/yr developer account, weeks of review, and nobody
  downloads an app for one cafe. A PWA does 95% of it for 0% of the cost.
- **Deleting Talabat.** Talabat is discovery. His site is retention. He needs both. Never
  frame this as replacing Talabat — frame it as *not paying commission on customers who
  already know him.*
- **A loyalty scheme with points and tiers.** Stamps only. Complexity kills adoption.

## 3. Pricing

### What to quote

Show him three options on one page. People pick the middle one. Design the middle one to be
what you actually want.

| | **Starter** | **Standard** ← aim here | **Complete** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bilingual menu site | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| QR codes | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Owner edits prices | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Pickup + drive-thru ordering | — | ✓ | ✓ |
| Kitchen screen | — | ✓ | ✓ |
| Card + Apple Pay | — | — | ✓ |
| Loyalty stamps | — | — | ✓ |
| Sales dashboard | — | — | ✓ |
| **One-time** | **AED 2,500** | **AED 5,500** | **AED 9,500** |

Plus a **Care Plan at AED 250/month** (or AED 2,500/year paid upfront) covering hosting,
domain, SSL, backups, security updates, and up to 2 hours of changes a month. Anything
beyond that is AED 150/hour.

### Why these numbers

- Abu Dhabi agencies quote AED 8,000–25,000 for a restaurant ordering system. You're
  materially under that, which is your advantage as a solo student — say so plainly rather
  than pretending you're an agency.
- Below AED 2,500 you signal "this is a school project" and he'll treat it like one.
- The Care Plan matters more than the build fee. AED 250 × 12 = AED 3,000/year of income
  that doesn't require you to find a new client.

### If he flinches at the number

Offer the split, not a discount:

> **AED 3,000 now + AED 500/month for 12 months.** Total AED 9,000, and he gets the
> Complete tier.

This is better for you (AED 9,000 > AED 5,500) and better for him (AED 3,000 out of pocket).
Small business owners think in monthly cash flow, not project totals.

### The commission argument — do the maths in front of him

Don't guess his numbers. Ask, then compute on paper:

> "How many orders a day come through Talabat, roughly? And what's your average ticket?
> And what percentage do they take?"

Then: `orders/day × avg ticket × commission% × 30` = his monthly commission bill.
If even a fifth of those customers order direct instead, the site pays for itself in
month one or two. Let *him* say the numbers so it's his conclusion, not your sales pitch.

### Do NOT

- Do not offer to build it free "for the portfolio." He'll value it at exactly what he paid.
- Do not take payment in coffee.
- Do not start building before you have a signed one-page agreement and a **50% deposit**.
  This is the most common way first freelance projects end badly.

## 4. Publishing it — steps and real costs

### Recurring costs

| Item | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Domain `.com` | ~AED 50 / year | Cloudflare or Namecheap, at cost |
| Domain `.ae` | ~AED 250–400 / year | Only if he specifically wants `.ae`; needs a trade licence |
| Server (VPS) | ~AED 15–25 / month | Hetzner CX22 or DigitalOcean basic droplet. A Go binary + SQLite is tiny. |
| DNS + CDN + SSL | AED 0 | Cloudflare free plan; Let's Encrypt via Caddy |
| Backups | ~AED 5 / month | Nightly SQLite dump to Backblaze B2 |
| Transactional email | AED 0 | Resend/Postmark free tiers cover a small cafe |
| **Running total** | **~AED 40–90 / month** | This is what you actually pay |

You charge AED 250/month for the Care Plan. That margin is your payment for being on call —
don't feel bad about it, that *is* the product.

### Payment gateway (Phase 2 only)

- Stripe UAE: roughly **2.9% + AED 1 per transaction**, no monthly fee, no setup fee.
- Local alternatives (Telr, PayTabs, Network International N-Genius, Tap): often lower
  percentages but with setup fees and monthly minimums.
- **Apple Pay is not a separate product.** It comes free with Stripe once the domain is
  verified. Don't let him think you're charging him extra for it.
- **The account must be in the cafe's name**, opened by the owner with his trade licence,
  Emirates ID and bank details. You cannot open it for him and you should not touch his
  banking credentials. Approval typically takes days to a few weeks.

> ⚠️ All figures above are indicative and change. Check current pricing on each provider's
> site the day before your meeting so you're not quoting stale numbers to his face.

### Deployment steps, in order

1. **Register the domain** — in *his* name, on *his* account, paid with *his* card. You get
   admin access. This single decision prevents 90% of freelancer-client disputes.
2. **Point DNS at Cloudflare**, enable proxying and full-strict SSL.
3. **Provision the VPS**, Ubuntu LTS, create a non-root user, key-only SSH, enable ufw.
4. **Install Caddy** as a reverse proxy — it handles HTTPS certificates automatically with
   no configuration. Two lines in a Caddyfile.
5. **Deploy** — `GOOS=linux go build`, scp the binary, run it as a systemd service with
   `Restart=always`.
6. **Nightly backup** — cron job dumping SQLite to Backblaze, and *test a restore once*
   before you tell him backups exist.
7. **Uptime monitoring** — UptimeRobot free tier, alerts to your phone.
8. **Google Search Console + sitemap**, then add the website link to his **Google Business
   Profile** and Instagram bio. This step drives more traffic than anything else on the list
   and takes ten minutes.
9. **Print the QR codes** — one per table, one at the counter, one at the drive-thru window,
   each with its own tracking code. Vinyl or acrylic stands, not paper.
10. **Train the staff** — 20 minutes on the kitchen screen and the sold-out toggle. Leave a
    one-page laminated cheat sheet in Arabic and English. If the staff don't use it, the
    project fails no matter how good the code is.

## 5. Questions he will ask — and your answers

**"Why do I need this? I have Talabat."**
Talabat brings you customers you don't have. This keeps the ones you already have. Every
regular who orders through Talabat instead of directly costs you commission on a sale you'd
have made anyway.

**"How much?"**
Give the three-tier sheet. Don't say a number before showing the sheet.

**"That's expensive."**
Compare to his commission bill, not to other websites. Then offer the monthly split.

**"Can I change prices myself?"**
Yes — that's built in from day one. You log in from your phone, change a price, it's live
in two seconds. Same for marking something sold out.

**"What if it breaks at 11pm on a Friday?"**
Be honest: you're one person, not a support desk. Commit to something you can actually
keep — e.g. "I respond within 4 hours during opening hours, and the site failing over means
you take orders the way you do now. Nothing about your current setup breaks."

**"You're studying in Canada. What happens when you go back?"**
Don't dodge this — he will ask, and getting caught improvising looks bad. Decide your answer
now. Options: the Care Plan continues remotely (you're a developer, time zones are a
non-issue for a system that runs itself); you hand over full documentation and source code so
he's never locked in; and you name a realistic response window given the time difference.
A Toronto–Abu Dhabi gap is 8 hours — his 3 PM opening is 7 AM your time, which is actually
workable. Say that. It turns an objection into a specific plan.

**"Who owns the website?"**
He does. Domain in his name, code handed over, no lock-in. Say this before he asks — it's
the most reassuring thing you can offer and it costs you nothing.

**"How long?"**
Phase 1 in 2–3 weeks from the day you get his real price list and photos. Be clear that
*his* delays (photos, prices, feedback) push the date, not yours.

**"Is it secure? What about my customers' cards?"**
Card data never touches your server. Stripe handles it — the same company Amazon and
Shopify merchants use. You store an ID, nothing more.

**"Do I need anything from my side?"**
Yes, and get this list to him in writing:
- Current price list, confirmed and complete
- Trade licence (only for the payment gateway, Phase 2)
- Logo files, ideally vector
- Photos — good ones. His Drivu menu has 30+ items with no photo at all, just the logo.
- An old tablet for the kitchen screen
- Two hours of a staff member's time for training

**"Can you do my Instagram too?"**
Say no, or quote it separately and honestly. Social media management is a different job with
a different time commitment and it will quietly eat the profit out of the web project.

**"Can you add [thing] as well?"**
"Yes — that's a Phase 2/3 item, let's get this live first." Never say no to a feature; say
*not yet*. Scope creep is what kills freelance projects.

**"My cousin can do it for AED 800."**
Let him. Say: "He might. What I'd ask is who fixes it when it breaks, and who owns it if he
stops answering." Don't compete on price with someone's cousin — you'll lose and it's not a
client you want.

### Things to ask *him*

Turn up with these. Asking good questions signals competence faster than any portfolio.

1. How many orders a day, and what's your average ticket?
2. What percentage do Talabat and Drivu take?
3. What's the split between dine-in, drive-thru and delivery?
4. What's your busiest hour?
5. What do customers ask for that you don't sell?
6. Do you plan to open a second branch? *(Changes the architecture — build multi-branch-ready if yes.)*
7. Who on your staff will actually use the tablet?
8. Do you have professional food photos, or do we need to shoot them?
9. Is there VAT registration in place, and do your listed prices include it?
10. Do you have Instagram admin access, or does someone else run it?

## 6. Be honest about these

Overpromising on your first client is how people end up working for free for six months.

- **Apple Pay depends on him**, not you. He has to open the merchant account with his trade
  licence. If his licence doesn't cover online sales, that's an amendment and a delay. Say
  this at the first meeting, not the fourth.
- **You cannot enter his card, bank or Emirates ID details.** He does that himself, on his own
  accounts. Say so — it reads as professional, not limiting.
- **Claude will generate a lot of this fast, but not all of it correctly.** Budget real time
  for reading, testing and fixing. A generated app that looks finished is not a shipped app.
  Don't quote a timeline based on how fast the first version appears.
- **UAE data protection law** (Federal Decree-Law 45/2021) applies to storing customer names
  and phone numbers. You need a basic privacy policy on the site. This is a page of text, not
  a crisis — but don't collect data you don't need.
- **Get 50% upfront and a one-page scope in writing.** One page is enough: what's included,
  what isn't, timeline, payment schedule, who owns what, how changes are priced.
